# AI Opportunity Roadmap: buyer pain inventory

Source list for `/roadmap` and outbound copy. Use the strongest pains on the page. Keep the rest for email, LinkedIn, and one-pagers.

ICP: owners, executives, and division leaders at $10M+ companies with complex quoting or bidding from drawings (commercial building-product manufacturers such as railings and fall protection, metal panels, louvers, doors and frames, storefront, millwork), plus other manual or knowledge-work-heavy firms.

Tone when using these: plain language, short sentences, second person ("you"). No invented stats. No client names. Never the word "audit". No em-dashes.

---

## Founder-confirmed pains (must use)

1. It takes too long to get customers their quotes.
2. It takes too long to get new hires up to speed.
3. Your people are stuck on busy work instead of real customer work.
4. The deals you can chase are limited by the hours you have.
5. You know there is a lot more capacity in the business if you could unlock it.
6. You are losing customers because competitors answer faster.
7. A regulation or deadline means something has to get done right now.

---

## Speed and lost deals

- Bids due Friday and the take-off is still open.
- Quotes sit in a queue waiting on one estimator.
- Customers call twice asking where their number is.
- You submit late and already know you are out.
- RFQs pile up on Monday and you cherry-pick what you can finish.
- The bid window closed while drawings were still being measured.
- Sales promises a 48-hour turnaround you cannot keep.
- You win the walkthrough and lose the bid on response time.
- Competitors send a priced package while you are still marking up PDFs.
- Hot jobs get attention. Everything else waits, then dies.

## Key-person risk and tribal knowledge

- Pricing rules live in one senior rep's head.
- When that person is out, quoting slows to a crawl.
- You are one resignation away from a quoting crisis.
- The best estimator is also the hardest person to replace.
- Estimators are hard to hire and harder to keep.
- New people ask the same questions every week.
- Spec exceptions are tribal knowledge, not a system.
- Only one person knows which products substitute cleanly.
- Code and product rules are scattered across emails and sticky notes.
- Training a replacement takes months you do not have.

## Errors and margin

- Mispriced quotes eat margin after you win.
- Missed line items show up as change orders and awkward calls.
- Rework burns hours you already billed once in your head.
- Take-off mistakes turn into job-site surprises.
- Discounting happens because nobody trusts the first number.
- You pad quotes to cover uncertainty, then lose on price.
- Hand math and copy-paste between spreadsheets create silent errors.
- Specs get misread and the wrong product ships into the estimate.
- Margin looks fine until the job is half done.
- You find out which quotes were wrong only after the customer pushes back.

## Capacity and growth ceilings

- You turn down work because the estimating bench is full.
- Hiring does not keep up with inbound RFQs.
- Revenue is capped by how many hours your estimators have.
- Growth plans assume capacity you cannot create with people alone.
- The sales team can sell more than operations can quote.
- Backlog is healthy. Throughput is not.
- Expanding into a new region means the same bottleneck, just farther away.
- You already know the next hire will not fix the queue for long.
- Peak season breaks the process every year the same way.
- Parent company wants growth. Your quoting process says no.

## Team burnout and overtime

- Estimators stay late every bid deadline week.
- Weekends get eaten by take-offs and revisions.
- Good people burn out and start looking.
- Morale drops when the queue never clears.
- Overtime becomes the plan instead of the exception.
- Your best people spend nights on work a system should draft.
- Vacation coverage means someone else's overtime.
- The team ships quotes tired, then owns the mistakes.

## Owner and executive bottleneck

- Everything still needs your sign-off.
- Pricing exceptions land on your desk at 6pm.
- You are the escalation path for every hard bid.
- Division leaders cannot move without you in the loop.
- You travel and quoting freezes until you answer email.
- The business waits on your judgment for work that is mostly pattern matching.
- You cannot step back because the process depends on you.
- Board or ownership asks for scale. You are still in the weeds.

## Onboarding time

- New estimators take too long to become useful.
- Ramp time costs you deals before the hire pays off.
- Shadowing the senior person is the only training plan.
- Playbooks are thin. Real learning is on the job, slowly.
- You hire ahead of demand and still feel behind for months.
- Contract or temp help cannot price your work safely.
- Cross-training never happens because everyone is quoting.

## Compliance and regulation deadlines

- A new code cycle or spec update lands and everything changes at once.
- Documentation has to be right, and right now.
- Submittals and compliance packs slow the quote.
- A customer or GC needs proof before they will even look at price.
- Regulatory deadlines do not care about your backlog.
- You scramble to update pricing rules when standards shift.
- Division rollouts need consistent compliance language across sites.

## AI confusion and pressure

- Vendors pitch tools. Nobody maps them to your real work.
- You do not know where to start without wasting money.
- You are afraid to buy software that sits unused.
- A parent company or board asks what your AI plan is.
- Peer companies talk about AI and you have nothing concrete to say.
- IT wants a platform. Ops wants a quote out the door.
- You have been burned by a pilot that never reached production.
- Everyone has an opinion. Nobody has a ranked list with dollar impact.
- You need a plan written from your side of the table, not a vendor's.

## Visibility

- You do not know your true win rate by job type.
- You cannot see where estimating time actually goes.
- Quote cycle time is a feeling, not a number.
- You guess which products or regions drain margin.
- Leadership meetings rehash anecdotes instead of a clear funnel.
- You cannot tell which steps are slow versus which steps are broken.
- Nobody can show what one more estimator would unlock in revenue.
- Reporting lives in spreadsheets that disagree with each other.

## Competitive and customer pressure

- Customers expect priced answers in hours, not days.
- National competitors answer faster with thinner teams.
- Buying groups and GCs route work to whoever responds first.
- You lose on speed and tell yourself it was price.
- Repeat customers quietly try someone else for the rush jobs.
- Your reputation for quality does not protect slow quotes.

## Process sprawl and tool friction

- Drawings, emails, ERP, CRM, and spreadsheets do not talk to each other.
- The same data gets typed three times.
- Version control on bid packages is a folder mess.
- Status lives in someone's inbox.
- Handoffs between estimating, sales, and operations drop context.
- Every "quick fix" spreadsheet becomes another system of record.

## Division and group dynamics

- One division has a working process. The others do not.
- Corporate wants a group AI story. Divisions need local wins first.
- Pilots stall because nobody owns the rollout after the demo.
- You need a plan that works in one plant and can spread.
- Sister companies ask to copy what you did, and you have nothing written down.

Use these on the public page. Keep the rest for outbound.

6. New hires take too long to ramp.
7. You turn down or soft-pedal work because capacity is stuck.

## Outbound one-liners (optional)

- How long does a drawing set sit before it becomes a priced quote?
- If your top estimator left next month, how long until quoting is normal again?
- How many RFQs did you not chase last quarter because the bench was full?
- When a bid is due Friday, who stays late, and what still slips?
- If the parent company asks for your AI plan tomorrow, what do you send?
